### **Complete Accounting Journal System**

The system provides **5 essential journal types** to properly categorize all your business transactions:

#### **1. Sales Journal (SJ)**
- Record project billings and revenue
- Link to customers/projects
- Track payment terms
- Generate sales reports

#### **2. Cash Receipt Journal (CR)**
- Record all cash and check receipts
- Customer payments tracking
- Bank deposit reconciliation
- Apply payments to invoices

#### **3. Accounts Payable Journal (APJ)**
- Record vendor invoices
- Track amounts owed
- Payment due dates
- Vendor statements

#### **4. Disbursement Journal (DJ)**
- Record all cash payments
- Check register functionality
- Vendor payments
- Expense reimbursements

#### **5. General Journal (GJ)**
- Adjusting entries
- Depreciation entries
- Transfer between accounts
- Accruals and deferrals
- Correction entries

### **Powerful Financial Reports**

#### 1. Trial Balance
**Purpose:** Verify the accuracy of your double-entry bookkeeping

**Features:**
- View trial balance as of any date
- Shows all accounts with debit and credit balances
- Verifies debits = credits
- Identifies posting errors quickly
- Export to Excel for detailed analysis
- Print for documentation

**Use it to:**
- Verify monthly closing accuracy
- Prepare for financial statement generation
- Audit account balances
- Find posting errors before they become problems

#### 2. General Ledger
**Purpose:** See transaction-level detail for every account

**Features:**
- Transaction-by-transaction listing
- Running balance for each account
- Filter by date range
- View by month or custom period
- Shows journal source (SJ, CR, APJ, DJ, GJ)
- Export individual ledgers or all accounts
- Drill-down to source transaction

**Use it to:**
- Investigate specific account activity
- Reconcile accounts
- Verify transaction coding
- Support audit requirements
- Trace transaction flow

#### 3. Income Statement (Profit & Loss)
**Purpose:** Understand your company's profitability

**Features:**
- Revenue summary by account
- Expense breakdown by category
- Gross profit calculation

- Net income/loss calculation

- Export to Excel for board presentations

- **Operating Expenses** - Overhead, salaries, rent, utilities

**Use it to:**

- Identify cost overruns

#### 4. Balance Sheet
**Purpose:** See your company's financial position at a point in time

**Features:**

- Liabilities breakdown (current and long-term)
- Equity section with retained earnings
- As-of-date reporting
- Comparative periods
- Export to Excel
- Print for loan applications

**Shows you:**
- **Assets** - Cash, receivables, equipment, property
- **Liabilities** - Payables, loans, accrued expenses
- **Equity** - Owner's capital, retained earnings
- **Financial Ratios** - Calculate current ratio, debt-to-equity

**Use it to:**
- Assess company financial health
- Apply for loans or credit
- Attract investors

## 📊 Audit & Compliance

### **Comprehensive Audit Trail**
Every change to every record is tracked:
- ✅ **What changed** - Field-level tracking (old → new values)
- ✅ **Who changed it** - User attribution
- ✅ **When it changed** - Timestamp
- ✅ **Why it changed** - Required reason/notes

### **Approval Workflows**
Quality control through multi-step approvals:
- **Draft** - Entry created but not posted
- **Submit** - Entry submitted for review
- **Approve** - Supervisor approves and posts
- **Posted** - Entry affects account balances

### **Change Request System**
For corrections to posted entries:
1. User requests change to posted entry
2. System tracks old values and proposed new values
3. Admin reviews and approves/rejects
4. If approved, change is applied with full audit trail
5. Original entry history is preserved

### **Cancellation Controls**
Proper handling of cancelled entries:
- Soft delete (entry remains in database)
- Cancellation reason required
- Cancellation date and user tracked
- Cancelled entries excluded from reports
- Original data preserved for audit

## 💡 Typical Construction Company Workflows

### **Scenario 1: Recording a Project Invoice**

**You billed a customer for completed work:**

1. **Create Sales Journal Entry**
   - Debit: Accounts Receivable - ABC Construction
   - Credit: Project Revenue - Residential Building
   - Amount: ₱500,000
   - Description: "Progress billing - Foundation work completed"
   - Attach: Invoice #2024-001

2. **Submit for Approval**
   - Bookkeeper submits entry
   - Accountant reviews and approves

3. **Entry is Posted**
   - Accounts Receivable increases by ₱500,000
   - Revenue recognized
   - Appears in Trial Balance
   - Flows to Income Statement

### **Scenario 2: Paying a Vendor Invoice**

**Received materials invoice from supplier:**

1. **Create Accounts Payable Entry**
   - Debit: Materials Expense
   - Credit: Accounts Payable - Steel Supplier Inc.
   - Amount: ₱250,000
   - Description: "Rebar delivery - Project ABC"
   - Reference: Vendor Invoice #12345

2. **Entry Posted After Approval**
   - Expense recorded
   - Liability created
   - Shows in Payables report

3. **Pay the Invoice**
   - Create Disbursement Journal Entry
   - Debit: Accounts Payable - Steel Supplier Inc.
   - Credit: Cash in Bank
   - Amount: ₱250,000
   - Check #: 5001

4. **Liability Cleared**
   - Payable reduced to zero
   - Cash reduced
   - Shows in ledger with full payment history

### **Scenario 3: Month-End Closing**

**Preparing monthly financial statements:**

1. **Review Pending Entries**
   - Approve all submitted entries
   - Verify all transactions for the month are entered

2. **Make Adjusting Entries (General Journal)**
   - Depreciation on equipment
   - Accrued expenses (utilities, salaries)
   - Prepaid expense adjustments
   - Revenue recognition adjustments

3. **Generate Trial Balance**
   - Verify debits = credits
   - Check for unusual balances
   - Review account totals

4. **Generate Financial Statements**
   - Run Income Statement for the month
   - Review revenue and expenses
   - Calculate net profit/loss
   - Run Balance Sheet as of month-end
   - Verify asset = liability + equity

5. **Export and Distribute**
   - Export statements to Excel
   - Format for presentation
   - Share with management
   - File for tax preparation

6. **Archive Documentation**
   - Print journal entries
   - Print financial statements
   - File supporting documents

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### **Q: Do I need an IT person to run this?**
A: No. Once installed, the system is user-friendly for accountants and bookkeepers. Basic computer skills are sufficient.

### **Q: Can I import my existing chart of accounts?**
A: Yes. We provide Excel templates for bulk import. You can set up your entire chart of accounts in minutes.

### **Q: How many users can use the system?**
A: Unlimited users. No per-user fees. Set up as many user accounts as needed.

### **Q: Can I track multiple projects?**
A: The current system uses customer/project fields and descriptions to tag entries. A full project accounting module can be developed and integrated based on your requirements.

### **Q: What if I make a mistake in a posted entry?**
A: Use the change request system to propose corrections. An admin reviews and approves the change with full audit trail.

### **Q: Does it handle payroll?**
A: Payroll is not currently built-in, but can be developed and integrated. The system can accommodate payroll summary entries in the General Journal while the module is being developed.

### **Q: Does it handle foreign currency?**
A: The system is designed for Philippine Peso. Foreign currency transactions can be recorded at converted amounts with notes.

### **Q: Can I give my accountant view-only access?**
A: Yes. Create a user account with "View-Only" role. They can see all data and reports but cannot modify anything.

### **Q: How do I backup my data?**
A: Simple file-based backup. Just copy the database file to a backup location. We recommend daily backups.

### **Q: What happens during an audit?**
A: Generate audit reports, print journal entries, export ledgers to Excel. Complete audit trail shows who did what and when.
